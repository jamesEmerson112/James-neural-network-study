The question changed again
Same map, now water mains. Each pipe has a capacity and the pumps make it one-way. How many kL per minute can the Plant deliver to the Harbor at once, using every pipe in parallel? Swap water for people and it is an evacuation plan.
Augmenting paths
Edmonds–Karp is Ford–Fulkerson with one rule added: always take the fewest-pipe augmenting path, found by BFS on the residual graph. That rule is what stops it looping forever on awkward capacities.

Where the two trees part
Both grow a tree outward from one root, both pop the smallest key, both relax every edge of the site they just took. Dijkstra measures distance from the root; Prim measures the edge in front of it. Nine of the eleven lines are identical.

Why the tree is enough
Run Prim but pop the largest key. Every site joins through the heaviest link that reaches it, so the unique tree path between any two sites is a widest path: no other route can beat its bottleneck. One tree answers the question for all 45 pairs at once.

The question
A convoy must leave the Plant for the Harbor on fully independent routes — no two lorries on the same road, so one closure can never strand two of them. How many such routes exist? Nothing about this smells like water.
Routes extracted
0independent routes = max flow with every capacity 1
The trick
Set every capacity to 1. A unit of flow can now only be a whole route, and two units can never share a pipe, so the max flow value is the number of edge-disjoint routes — and the min cut is the smallest set of roads that severs them all (Menger’s theorem).
Same trick, different clothes
Bipartite matching. Workers on the left, jobs on the right, an edge where a worker can do a job. Add a source into every worker and a sink out of every job, give every edge capacity 1, and the maximum matching is the max flow. Scheduling, ad auctions and organ-exchange programs all arrive here.
